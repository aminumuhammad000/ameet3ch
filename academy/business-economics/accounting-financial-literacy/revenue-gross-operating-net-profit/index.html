Demystifying Profit: Revenue, Gross, Operating, and Net Profit Margins

12 min Beginner to Intermediate Updated 2026 Free Public Lesson

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish Revenue from Gross, Operating, and Net Profit.
  • Compute Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and Operating Expenses (OPEX).
  • Calculate profit margins versus markups without mathematical error.
  • Analyze a multi-step Income Statement with practical African business metrics.

1. The Four Tiers of the Income Statement

A commercial organization records earnings across four distinct tiers:

1. **Gross Revenue (Top Line):** Total invoice value or cash received from goods sold and services delivered before any deductions. 2. **Gross Profit:** Revenue minus direct Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). Measures production efficiency and product unit margins. 3. **Operating Profit (EBIT):** Gross Profit minus operational expenditures (OPEX) such as administrative salaries, rent, software, and marketing. Measures core business profitability. 4. **Net Profit (The Bottom Line):** Operating Profit minus finance expenses (interest on debt), foreign exchange losses, and corporate income taxes. Represents the actual profit belonging to equity owners.

2. The Mathematical Formulas

Formula:
\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS}$$ $$\text{Gross Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times 100\%
Formula:
\text{Operating Profit (EBIT)} = \text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses (OPEX)}$$ $$\text{Operating Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Operating Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times 100\%
Formula:
\text{Net Profit} = \text{Operating Profit} - \text{Interest} - \text{Taxes} - \text{Extraordinary Losses}$$ $$\text{Net Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times 100\%

3. Markup vs. Margin: The Critical Distinction

Confusing **Markup** with **Margin** is one of the most fatal financial blunders made by growing SME operators:

  • Markup:** The percentage added directly to the Cost of Goods Sold to arrive at the selling price:
  • $$\text{Markup} = \frac{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Cost}}{\text{Cost}} \times 100\%$$
  • Margin:** The percentage of the final selling price that remains as profit:
  • $$\text{Margin} = \frac{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Cost}}{\text{Selling Price}} \times 100\%$$
  • Worked Comparison:**
  • If you buy an item for ₦6,000 and sell it for ₦10,000:
  • Profit = ₦4,000
  • $\text{Markup} = \frac{4,000}{6,000} = 66.67\%$
  • $\text{Margin} = \frac{4,000}{10,000} = 40.00\%$
  • Warning:* A 50% markup is NOT a 50% margin. If your retail cost is ₦1,000 with a 50% markup, you sell at ₦1,500. Your margin is $\frac{500}{1,500} = 33.3\%$. If overheads are 40% of sales, you are losing money on every unit!

Key Takeaways

  • Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, cash flow is reality.
  • Gross margin measures manufacturing and fulfillment efficiency.
  • Operating margin measures operational leverage and overhead control.
  • Markup is calculated on cost; margin is calculated on selling price.

Knowledge Check

Test your understanding of the concepts covered in this lesson:

Question 1: A merchant buys a gadget for ₦15,000 and sells it for ₦20,000. What is the merchant's gross profit margin?
Explanation: Gross profit = ₦20,000 - ₦15,000 = ₦5,000. Gross Margin = (₦5,000 / ₦20,000) * 100% = 25.0%. (The markup on cost is 33.3%).
