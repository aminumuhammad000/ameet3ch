HER THRIVE 2026: Three Days of Digital Empowerment, Learning and Collaboration

From 5 to 7 October 2026, young women gathered at Pioneers ICT in Kano, Nigeria, for HER THRIVE, an intensive three-day digital entrepreneurship training programme designed to build practical technical competencies, financial discipline, and collaborative business confidence. Here is a firsthand look into how our team guided participants from digital fundamentals to live pitch presentations.

Aminu Muhammad Facilitator & Organising Team Member • Pioneers ICT
Event Dates: 5–7 October 2026 Pioneers ICT, Kano, Nigeria

1. Introduction: A Meaningful Three-Day Experience

Between 5 and 7 October 2026, I had the privilege of serving as a facilitator and member of the organising team for the HER THRIVE programme, hosted at Pioneers ICT in Kano, Nigeria. HER THRIVE was designed as a dedicated three-day digital entrepreneurship training programme for young women, focused on practical digital capabilities, business branding, and financial tracking under the theme Build, Brand, Grow.

Throughout Northern Nigeria and across the country, increasing numbers of young women are venturing into enterprise, from culinary arts and fashion to creative media and online commerce. However, transforming an idea into a sustainable digital enterprise requires structured foundational skills: understanding online account safety, establishing digital presence, managing money responsibly, and communicating business value clearly.

HER THRIVE brought together enthusiastic participants, dedicated trainers, and organisers in a collaborative classroom environment. As both a facilitator and member of the organising team, my responsibility was to ensure our curriculum remained grounded in real-world utility, making complex digital and business concepts approachable, actionable, and immediately applicable.

2. Preparing for HER THRIVE

A successful community training event begins well before the first participant walks through the door. Effective delivery demands thorough preparation, close coordination among the organising team, and careful technical setup of the learning space.

Our priority was to establish a supportive, inclusive, and distraction-free learning environment. This involved preparing the physical workstations, arranging reliable power connections and extension boards for laptops, verifying internet connectivity, and testing presentation displays so that every attendee had an unobstructed view of live software demonstrations.

Beyond hardware and space planning, the organising team coordinated the curriculum flow across the three days. We structured the sequence logically so that digital setup and account safety preceded branding, and branding preceded commercial record keeping and pitch presentations. This deliberate sequencing ensured that participants could continuously build upon what they practiced the previous morning.

3. Day One: Digital Setup and Getting Started

Day One focused on building the digital foundation. Many aspiring entrepreneurs interact with the digital economy primarily through social media feeds on mobile devices, often without having set up professional email infrastructure or robust security measures.

My primary facilitation role during this opening day was guiding participants through essential digital setup tasks. We walked through:

  • Setting Up and Accessing Email Accounts: Explaining why a dedicated business email is necessary for registering services, securing commercial communications, and separating business operations from personal messaging.
  • Understanding Account Security: Explaining how passwords are compromised, why reusing identical credentials across multiple apps poses severe risks, and how to create strong, memorable passphrases.
  • Enabling Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Demonstrating step-by-step how two-factor authentication works as a secondary safety shield via SMS or authenticator apps to prevent unauthorized account access.
  • Basic Digital Safety Practices: Educating participants on identifying phishing attempts, suspect links on messaging apps, and suspicious requests for OTP codes.
  • Building Device Confidence: Helping participants navigate desktop and browser interfaces so they can operate digital tools with greater independence.

Our focus was on demystifying these concepts for learners of all backgrounds. Rather than presenting abstract cybersecurity theory, we demonstrated real scenarios: what happens when an email recovery phone number is missing, or how a single stolen login can compromise an entire business page.

4. Learning Through Practical Sessions

Passive lectures rarely produce confident digital creators. At HER THRIVE, the core educational philosophy was experiential, hands-on practice. After every brief presentation, participants immediately opened their laptops or smartphones to perform the exercises themselves.

When learners encountered hurdles (whether a browser setting, a profile registration error, or a software navigation prompt), facilitators and co-trainers were right beside them. Facilitator Fatima Sulaiman Muhammad provided dedicated one-on-one assistance to attendees, helping them troubleshoot mobile and branding steps. Facilitator Hafiz Makama worked directly with participants around the U-shaped table to guide device configurations and practical exercises.

The classroom dynamic quickly transitioned from quiet concentration into vibrant peer-to-peer collaboration. Participants helped their neighbors locate tools, shared device screens, and discussed branding ideas as sessions covered visual identity, logo concepts, and business positioning.

5. Day Three: Payments and Record Keeping

On Day Three, I facilitated the dedicated session on Payments and Record Keeping. In my experience working with startups and growing enterprises across Kano and Nigeria, poor financial record keeping is one of the most common reasons early-stage businesses struggle to survive.

Many micro-entrepreneurs fall into the trap of confusing gross sales with real profit. If a trader collects ₦100,000 from customers but spent ₦70,000 on stock, logistics, and packaging, their profit is ₦30,000 (not ₦100,000). Spending out of total cash collections often leads to depleted capital and cash flow distress.

During this session, we unpacked several critical business management principles:

  • Separating Personal and Business Finances: Why mixing business revenues with daily personal spending blinds the owner to actual profitability and makes restocking unpredictable.
  • The Core Profit Equation: Emphasizing that Profit = Sales − Expenses, and reinforcing that revenue in the bank account does not automatically equal profit to be withdrawn.
  • Recording Every Sale Immediately: Introducing simple, disciplined transaction logs containing the date, customer name, product sold, amount in Naira, and payment method (bank transfer vs. cash).
  • Tracking All Operational Expenses: Documenting material costs, transport, delivery fees, mobile data, packaging, and electricity to calculate true product margins.
  • Receiving Payments Professionally: Reviewing digital bank transfer verification, tracking unpaid customer balances (receivables), and issuing reliable payment confirmations.

6. Pitch Presentations and Teamwork

Technical literacy and record keeping gain true power when paired with clear communication. Toward the conclusion of HER THRIVE, participants transitioned into collaborative teamwork for pitch preparation and presentations.

I was actively involved in supporting Pitch Group B as teams organized their ideas, refined their value propositions, and practiced presenting to their peers. Table cards marked working teams such as Trendy Charm, Zarah Cakes & More, and Nafsa Delicacies, giving participants tangible ownership of their mock and emerging business concepts.

Presenting in front of an audience can be intimidating, especially for individuals who have never pitched a project before. The classroom served as a safe, encouraging testing ground. Participants articulated who their target customers were, what problem their service solved, how they intended to reach buyers online, and how they planned to manage their records. Facilitators provided constructive feedback on voice projection, clarity, and value communication.

7. Celebrating Participation and Achievement

The culmination of HER THRIVE was a celebratory closing ceremony recognizing each participant's commitment and perseverance throughout the three intensive days.

Every participant who completed the programme received an official Certificate of Participation from Pioneers ICT. These certificates marked active engagement and completion of the curriculum, honoring the time, energy, and dedication each young woman invested in expanding her digital horizons.

It was immensely rewarding to join fellow organisers and trainers in presenting certificates to participants including Aisha Muhammad Usman, Mubasshirah Asmau Abdulazeez, Chalya Mamdam, Fadila Muh'd Hamisu, Zainab Auwal Moh'd, Raji Muhammed Zainab, Nafisat Dayyabu Muh'd, and Khadija Dayyabu Muhammad.

The event also attracted regional media recognition, with coverage published by outlets such as Prime Time News detailing Pioneers ICT's initiative to train women in digital marketing and sustainable enterprise growth.

8. What I Learned From the Experience

Stepping into a training room as a facilitator and co-organiser always brings fresh perspectives. While building software products at Ameetech Technology Ltd involves writing code, configuring servers, and refining user interfaces, facilitating community-level digital training is a humbling reminder of where technology truly meets human progress.

Three central lessons resonated strongly with me throughout HER THRIVE:

  1. Foundational Basics Create Outsized Impact: Advanced tools are meaningless if basic access and account security remain fragile. Helping someone configure 2FA, recover an email safely, or log an expense accurately provides immediate, lasting protection.
  2. Facilitation Demands Patience and Clarity: Technical jargon often alienates learners. True mastery of technology is demonstrated by the ability to explain complex systems in simple, everyday language that empowers rather than intimidates.
  3. Collective Effort Outperforms Individual Brilliance: A cohesive team (where organisers handle logistics, trainers support each other's sessions, and participants encourage their peers) creates an atmosphere where everyone thrives.

9. Appreciation

A programme of this scale and impact is the product of sustained collaboration, thoughtful leadership, and genuine community commitment.

"As a member of the organising team, I am grateful for the planning, patience, coordination, teamwork, and commitment that made HER THRIVE possible. Everyone contributed in one way or another, from preparation to the actual event. I appreciate the ideas, time, support, and effort of everyone involved."

Special gratitude goes to the leadership of Pioneers ICT, founder Mallam Hassan, fellow facilitators, logistics coordinators, and every participant who brought their curiosity, time, and dedication to the classroom.

May Allah reward everyone for their efforts, bless the work of our hands, and open more doors for us to do greater things together. Ameen.

10. Conclusion

Empowering young women with practical digital literacy and entrepreneurship tools is not merely an educational goal; it is a vital catalyst for community resilience and economic progress in Kano and Northern Nigeria. When women acquire the skills to secure their digital identities, market their services, and maintain sound financial records, their businesses grow on solid foundations.

HER THRIVE 2026 was a deeply inspiring three days. At Ameetech Technology Ltd, we remain dedicated to advancing technology, supporting grassroots digital education, and contributing to initiatives that build local capability.
